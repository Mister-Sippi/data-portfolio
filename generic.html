About Me

Research-driven analysis for complex, messy systems.

I enjoy research and working with data, but what really drives me is understanding how complex systems actually work. During my PhD in Early Modern East Asian history, I constructed a dataset from unstructured Japanese judicial records and used it to challenge prevailing assumptions of static hegemony in Nagasaki. The work focused on extracting structure from fragmented sources to reveal how power operated in practice.

That same approach carried over into my work in CDD and compliance. Over three years, I analyzed both business and private clients by combining transaction data with internal records, public sources, and client statements. The objective was to identify patterns indicating financial risk and translate those signals into structured, defensible conclusions.

Across both contexts, the core work has been consistent: bringing structure to complex, incomplete information to expose underlying patterns. I apply this approach to messy datasets to identify what actually matters and translate quantitative signals into clear, defensible conclusions that improve how systems operate in practice.
